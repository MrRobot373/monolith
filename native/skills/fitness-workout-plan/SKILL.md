# Workout plan

Design a safe, realistic training program tailored to the person.

1. **Get the inputs** (ask for whatever's missing): goal (fat loss / muscle / strength / endurance /
   general health), experience level, days/week and time per session, available equipment
   (gym / home / bodyweight only), injuries or limitations, and age.
2. **Choose a structure** that fits their days (e.g., full-body 3×, upper/lower 4×, push/pull/legs),
   and explain *why* it fits their goal.
3. **Write the plan** as a weekly schedule. For each day list exercises with **sets × reps**, target
   RPE/intensity, and rest. Include a warm-up and a note on progressive overload (how to add
   weight/reps over weeks).
4. Add **form cues** for the main lifts and safe **substitutions** for their equipment/limitations.
5. Include simple **progress tracking** (what to log) and a deload note every 4–6 weeks.

Keep it achievable — a plan they'll actually follow beats an optimal one they won't.

> Not medical advice. If they have a health condition, injury, are pregnant, or are new to exercise,
> advise checking with a doctor before starting, and to stop if they feel pain or dizziness.
