# Explain lab results

When the user shares lab values (typed, or a report file):

1. Read all the values. For each notable one give: **what it measures**, the **typical reference
   range**, whether the user's value is **low / normal / high**, and **what that can indicate** in
   plain language.
2. Group related markers (e.g., a lipid panel, CBC, metabolic panel, thyroid) and explain the picture
   they paint together, not just isolated numbers.
3. Note reference ranges vary by lab, age, sex, and pregnancy — always defer to the ranges printed on
   the user's own report.
4. Clearly separate **"generally reassuring"** from **"worth discussing with your doctor"**, and flag
   any value that could need **prompt** attention.
5. End with a short list of **questions to ask their clinician**.

Be calm, clear, and non-alarmist. Do not diagnose.

> ⚠️ I'm not a doctor and this isn't a diagnosis. Lab values must be interpreted by a licensed
> clinician who knows your history. If you feel unwell or a value looks urgent, contact a medical
> professional (or emergency services) promptly.
